Crossover-Over

Highway 1 runs 0.93 miles through the middle of Golden Gate Park. This plan quiets it, bridges it, and eventually buries it, in four phases that each stand on their own.

The problem

A six-lane highway through the quietest part of the park

Crossover Drive and the Park Presidio Bypass carry State Route 1 from Lincoln Way at 19th Avenue to Fulton Street at Park Presidio, with a two-lane spur out to Fulton at 25th Avenue. Caltrans controls the road. Rec & Park controls everything around it.

Golden Gate Park is about three miles long and half a mile wide, so the highway cuts it at its waist. For about 1,850 feet between the MLK Drive signal and the JFK underpass there is no way across. Along Fulton, from Park Presidio to the bypass curve near 21st Avenue, there is none for over half a mile. The map below models where that traffic is loud enough to change what the park feels like.

Ground truth

The highway is a bench cut into Strawberry Hill

Walk the road in lidar and the shape is clear. North of MLK, the highway is cut into the west flank of Strawberry Hill: the ground rises 30–40 ft to Blue Heron Lake on one side and falls 20–40 ft toward Transverse Drive on the other. Along Fulton the bypass runs nearly level, slightly above the ground to the south. That shape decides every design below. A bridge can land on the hill at grade on the east side. A lid can tie into the hillside on the east and has to be built up on the west. An underpass works best along Fulton, where the road is already a little high.

The plan

Four phases, cheapest first

Each phase is useful even if the next one never happens, and each one makes the next easier to argue for. Costs are planning-level ranges in 2026 dollars, including design, Caltrans oversight and contingency. They are Class 5 estimates (AACE), which means the real number can land 50% lower or 100% higher. Their job is to tell you which conversation you are in.

0

Quiet Crossover: pavement and speed

The fastest way to give acres back to the park is to make the road itself quieter. At 35 mph most highway noise comes from tires on pavement. Rubberized open-graded asphalt is the quieter surface with the longest track record in Caltrans' own test sections. Dropping the posted limit from 35 to 30 mph lowers the source by about 1.7 dB in this model. Together that is roughly 4 dB, which moves the 55 dBA line in by a third.

If Caltrans already has SHOPP resurfacing programmed here, the extra cost is the difference in mix, not a new project.

1

The Crossover-Overs: one underpass, one bridge

Two crossings fix the two worst gaps.

1a · STA 10+83 · about 17th Ave

Fulton Grotto

A 16-ft-wide walking and biking underpass beneath the Park Presidio Bypass. The road sits a few feet above the park to the south, so the tunnel floor meets grade on the park side and a 5% ramp climbs to Fulton. It cuts the walk from Fulton & 17th Ave to the Blue Heron Lake boathouse from 1,212 m to about 834 m. It also follows the park's own tradition: the 1889 Alvord Lake Bridge, the first reinforced-concrete bridge in the country, is a pedestrian grotto under a park road.

1b · STA 38+06 · Strawberry Hill

Strawberry Hill Bridge

A walking and biking bridge from the Blue Heron Lake shore road to the Transverse Drive trail, crossing the highway between MLK and JFK. On the east the deck lands into the hillside at grade with no ramp. On the west it continues as a ramp-trail through the woods, about 60 ft down to Transverse at no more than 5%. The distance saved is modest, since MLK is close. The gain is a level, signal-free way across that doesn't drop you into the 6-lane intersection.

A 60-ft planted land bridge instead of a 16-ft deck would run $50–80M. Benchmarks: Palo Alto's Adobe Creek bike bridge over US-101 finished in 2021 at $23.7M. The Wallis Annenberg Wildlife Crossing, a 165-ft-wide planted bridge over ten lanes of US-101, is about $114M. Its funding is the model here: $25M from one foundation, more than $34M raised from philanthropy overall, the state for the rest.

One lesson from home: the 1962 pedestrian bridge at Geary and Steiner had spiral ramps at 11–17% grade and was demolished in 2020 because no one could use them in a wheelchair. Every approach here is drawn at 5% or flatter, so it counts as a walkway rather than an ADA ramp, with no handrails or landings required.

2

Strawberry Hill Lid

A 787-ft (240 m) concrete lid over both directions of traffic between MLK and JFK, planted on top and tied into the hillside. At that length the covered section is NFPA 502 Category A, so it needs no mechanical smoke ventilation. That keeps it a structure, not a tunnel with a control room. On the east the lid meets the hill almost at grade. On the west it is built up as a planted slope down toward Transverse Drive. Of every 240 m stretch tested, this location removes the most noisy acreage.

This is the scale of Presidio Tunnel Tops ($118M, mostly philanthropic). Deck-park costs elsewhere run $20M per acre for Klyde Warren in 2012 dollars, about $40M today, and an estimated $51M per acre for Atlanta's Stitch. The noise benefit alone is modest, about 15 acres, because the open road on either side keeps radiating. The reason to build it is the connection: 787 ft of hillside where you can walk straight from the lake into the western woods.

3

Crossover Tunnel

A cut-and-cover box, about 3,900 ft (1,190 m), from just north of the MLK signal to just west of Fulton and Park Presidio, built on the existing alignment so traffic keeps flowing during construction. The JFK Promenade keeps passing underneath through a longer grotto. Every lane stays: 71,000 trips a day still get through, and the park gets its waist back. After the Great Highway fight, that matters. Nothing here asks anyone to drive less.

At this length the tunnel is NFPA 502 Category D. That means emergency ventilation, fire detection and suppression, standby power and egress through the center wall, plus Caltrans staff to run it, at roughly $4–6M a year. The roof adds about 10 acres of new park surface. A cheaper variant, the String of Lids, is four 787-ft lids with short open gaps, each under the ventilation threshold. It gets about two-thirds of the noise benefit for roughly 60% of the cost, if a fire-life-safety review accepts the gaps.

Benchmark: the Presidio Parkway, which replaced Doyle Drive with two pairs of cut-and-cover tunnels of 850 and 1,020 ft plus viaducts, cost between $850M and $1.2B depending on what is counted. The site here has advantages. The road already sits in a bench, the dune sand above the groundwater is easy to excavate, and the box can be built half at a time behind temporary lanes. One cost goes up: there is no Doyle Drive-style seismic deficiency to pay for it. The money has to come from someone who wants the park back.

Dollars and cents

What each phase buys, at your assumptions

Park quiet doesn't trade on a market, so any dollar figure for it is an assumption. The calculator makes those assumptions visible. Defaults are deliberately conservative. Golden Gate Park averages about 23,600 visits per acre a year (24 million across 1,017 acres), and the default uses half that. The value of a quieter visit defaults to $4, below the bottom of the Army Corps' 2025 range for a general recreation day ($5.17–15.52).

How to read the result. Phase 0 pays for itself many times over at almost any assumption, so it should simply happen. Phase 1 is near break-even on time savings and new visits, and its real case is access for the Inner Richmond, which the calculator can't price. Phases 2 and 3 don't pass a conventional benefit-cost test, and the pitch shouldn't pretend they do. Their case is the land-equivalent column. Seventy-odd acres of quiet, central parkland can't be bought in San Francisco at any price, and the tunnel's cost is the same order as buying it if you could. That is a philanthropic argument and a hundred-year argument. Tunnel Tops and Klyde Warren were built on that argument.

Property value is left out on purpose. The model finds about 160 homes along Fulton and Lincoln that would get 1 dB or more quieter, but most already sit next to Fulton's own traffic. At standard hedonic rates (about 0.5% of value per dB) the total is roughly $3M. This is a park project.

Shovel-ready

The approvals path

Any work over, under or on Crossover is work on a state highway inside a National Register historic district. That fixes the cast. Caltrans owns the road. Rec & Park owns the park. The County Transportation Authority (SFCTA) is the natural sponsor of record for highway work. The Golden Gate Park nomination lists Crossover Drive and the Park Presidio Bypass among the park's 1930s public works, so historic review comes with it.

  1. Cooperative agreement and Project Initiation Document (Caltrans). SFCTA and Rec & Park sign on as sponsors and fund Caltrans oversight. Phase 0 and Phase 1 can be one PID. 6–12 months.
  2. Environmental clearance. California has a statutory CEQA exemption for public pedestrian and bicycle projects (Public Resources Code §21080.25) that currently runs to January 1, 2030. Phase 1 should be designed to fit it, and approved before it sunsets. Lids and the tunnel need a full EIR, and an EIS if federal money is used. Phase 1: months. Phases 2–3: 2–4 years.
  3. Historic resources review. Treat the WPA stonework and the JFK overcrossing as contributing features and design to the Secretary of the Interior's standards. A grotto and a land bridge fit the park's vocabulary better than a steel truss. Runs in parallel.
  4. Design and Caltrans encroachment permit. Plans, specifications and estimate, with traffic staging on a 71,000-car road. 12–24 months for Phase 1.
  5. Construction. Phase 1 structures are short, overnight-closure jobs. A lid or tunnel is built half at a time behind temporary lanes. Phase 1: 12–18 months.

The fastest real start is a $1–2M feasibility and PID study for Phases 0–1, funded privately and handed to SFCTA and Rec & Park. That is how Tunnel Tops and the Annenberg crossing began: donors paid for the plans, which made the public money easy to commit.

Funding

A gift sized to the moment

San Francisco's park philanthropy has a long run: Sutro planted Mount Sutro's forest and gave UC the Parnassus land in 1895, and Presidio Tunnel Tops raised $98M from donors. In November 2026 the city received the largest single gift in its history, and the mayor asked others to follow. Each phase here matches a kind of giver.

Phase 0 · $3–8M

Public. A letter from Rec & Park and the District 1 and 7 supervisors to Caltrans District 4, plus the incremental cost of quieter asphalt.

Phase 1 · $29–51M

One donor or a small group, or a project in the next round of the city's new parks gift. Two naming opportunities: the Grotto and the Bridge.

Phase 2 · $140–240M

A Tunnel Tops-style campaign run by a parks conservancy, with a lead gift naming the lid and the state covering structure in the highway right of way.

Phase 3 · $0.9–1.6B

State and federal highway money with a philanthropic match, or a city bond. A generational ask, and the one people would still be walking on in 2126.

Field check

Validate the noise model in an afternoon

The noise map is a model and should be checked against the park. A calibrated phone meter (NIOSH's SLM app with an external mic) or a Class 2 meter is enough.

  • Pick a transect perpendicular to the road at the Strawberry Hill Bridge site and along Fulton at 17th Ave.
  • Record 10-minute Leq at 50, 100, 200, 400 and 800 ft from the near lane, at about 5 ft above ground.
  • Repeat at 8 am, 1 pm and 5 pm on a weekday with light wind. Note wind and any planes, mowers or music.
  • Repeat at the same points on a quiet Sunday morning to get the park's background level.

Methods and sources

How the numbers were made

Geometry. OpenStreetMap ways for Highway 1, the spur, park paths, lakes and the park boundary, pulled November 20, 2026 and projected to UTM 10N. The park polygon measures 1,004 acres against the official 1,017. Both carriageways together run 2,988 m. Buffers are clipped to the park.

Elevation. USGS 3DEP bare-earth elevation at 2 m from the National Map. Lake levels are the median lidar return inside each shoreline. Elevations are NAVD88 feet.

Traffic. Caltrans 2024 AADT: 71,000 at Fulton (PM 5.00) and 68,000–71,000 at Lincoln (PM 4.05), peak hour 5,200, trucks 1.56% (68.5% two-axle). The spur is assumed at 8,000 a day at 25 mph. No count was found for it.

Noise. FHWA reference emission levels (RD-77-108) for autos, medium and heavy trucks at 38 mph. Average daytime hour is 80% of daily traffic over 15 hours. Each carriageway is broken into 5 m point sources with soft-ground excess attenuation (α = 0.5), summed on a 10 m grid. No credit is taken for foliage or terrain shielding, which would shrink the contours. The hard-ground sensitivity case gives the upper bound. Portals of covered sections re-radiate the energy of 30 m of open road, less 3 dB. Phase 0 is modeled as a 4 dB source reduction: about 1.7 dB from 30 mph and 2–3 dB from rubberized open-graded asphalt, which helps less at urban speeds than on freeways.

Walking distances. Shortest paths on the OSM walking network, excluding Highway 1 and the spur except at mapped crossings. Lid and tunnel cases assume free crossing with 30% circuity.

Costs. Parametric ranges from the benchmarks named above, escalated roughly with the Caltrans construction cost index. Every figure on this page is a planning estimate and none comes from an engineer's estimate.
