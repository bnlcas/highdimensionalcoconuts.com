# Getting Crossover-Over onto the city's agenda

Draft, November 21, 2026. Tags follow RESEARCH.md: [source], [verify] and, here, [judgment] for strategy calls.

## The short version

Rec & Park can't build this alone. Caltrans owns the road, and Caltrans works with a local sponsor, which in
San Francisco is normally the **County Transportation Authority (SFCTA)**. So the radar to get onto is SFCTA's
first, with Rec & Park and a supervisor as co-signers. The first ask is small and specific: **a funded study of the
Strawberry Hill and Fulton crossings.** Don't lead with lids, and never with a tunnel. [judgment]

Three things are open right now and won't stay open:

1. **Rec & Park is choosing how to spend $61M of the Chesky gift over the next year.** $1–2M for a feasibility
   study is a modest ask against that, and it's exactly how Tunnel Tops and the Annenberg crossing started. [source: KALW, Local News Matters]
2. **SFCTA's next long-range plan, SFTP 2060, starts in 2027.** Projects that aren't on its list have a hard
   time getting sales-tax (Prop L) or regional money for years afterward. [source: Geary/19th strategic case, §5.4]
3. **The CEQA exemption for walking and biking projects (PRC §21080.25) ends January 1, 2030.** The crossings
   should be approved before then. [source; verify eligibility]

## Who decides what

| Body | What it controls | Where this fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Caltrans District 4** | The road, encroachment permits, the project-development steps (PID → environmental → design → build) | Owner. Needs a sponsor and a cooperative agreement before it will spend staff time. |
| **SFCTA** (Board = the 11 supervisors) | Prop L sales tax, the Neighborhood Program (NTIP) planning grants, SFTP 2060, the Geary/19th subway study | Natural sponsor. Its Board took the Park Presidio undergrounding comment in April 2026. |
| **Rec & Park** (Commission, Capital Committee, staff) | Everything around the road; the remaining $61M of the Chesky gift; the Community Opportunity Fund | Co-sponsor and the face of it. Capital Committee takes public comment on items not on the agenda. [source] |
| **SFMTA** | Fulton Street signals and crossings, the 5 Fulton stops, the Golden Gate Park Access & Safety Program | The 22nd Ave spur fix, and the Fulton end of any crossing. |
| **Board of Supervisors** | District offices drive NTIP requests and SFCTA priorities | D1 (Connie Chan) holds the Richmond and much of the park. Check the official 2022 map for which districts touch Crossover at Lincoln; it may include D4 and D7. [verify] |
| **Mayor's office** | Asked other donors to follow Chesky | The philanthropic route runs through here. |

**On the supervisors.** Connie Chan (D1) stays on the Board through January 2029 after losing CA-11 to Scott Wiener.
Ben, a D1 constituent, expects little from her, and her record supports that: by Kids Safe SF's and Streetsblog's
accounts she lobbied against the JFK Promenade while taking no formal position on Prop J. She opposed Prop K. She
backed the 2026 effort to reopen the Great Highway on weekdays, and Kids Safe SF says she opposed the Geary red lanes
(one source). [source] Ask anyway, briefly and in writing, so the record shows the D1 office was asked. But don't
build the plan on her: **the NTIP request can come from any supervisor whose district touches the corridor, and the
SFCTA Board is all eleven.** [verify NTIP rules on which supervisor can sponsor]

- **Alan Wong (D4)** said after Prop G failed that he would still work on the traffic-safety and congestion concerns
  its backers raised. The Outer Sunset's cut-through traffic now runs up Chain of Lakes and 19th Avenue, through
  the park. That makes him the most natural first ally on the Board (see "Chain of Lakes" below). [source; judgment]
- **Myrna Melgar (D7)**, if the 2022 map puts the Lincoln end of Crossover in her district. [verify]
- **Scott Wiener** goes to Congress in January. House "Community Project Funding" (earmarks) can pay for a
  transportation study, a federal route that doesn't depend on USDOT's grant programs. He'll have bigger fights, so
  ask for something small and finished: a study earmark, or a letter of support. [verify the FY2028 earmark rules]
  His state senate seat is vacant; watch the special election.
- **Mayor Lurie** stood in Golden Gate Park to announce the Chesky gift and asked other donors to follow. The
  philanthropic route runs through his office and Rec & Park, not the Board. [source]

## The ladder, cheapest rung first

1. **A Neighborhood Program (NTIP) planning study, requested by a supervisor** (D4 or D7 if D1 won't). SFCTA gives each district
   planning money for neighborhood transportation studies. That's how the District 1 Multimodal Transportation
   Study (2024) was paid for, and it already covers this edge of the park. Ask for a follow-on: *"Crossover Drive
   crossings and Fulton Street park access."* This is the most realistic first yes. [source for the D1 study; verify current NTIP amounts]
2. **A $1–2M feasibility study funded privately or from the Chesky gift,** handed to SFCTA and Caltrans. That
   buys a Caltrans Project Initiation Document (a PSR-PDS), which is what "shovel-ready" starts with.
3. **The spur fix at 22nd Avenue** (raised crossing, a proper stop or signal at 25th). It's cheap and routine, and
   SFMTA and Caltrans can do it on their own schedule. Ask for it separately so it isn't held up by the rest.
4. **The Strawberry Hill crossing and the Fulton crossing**, funded by the state Active Transportation Program,
   Prop L and a donor. [verify the next ATP cycle dates]
5. **Lids, one at a time,** each with a donor's name on it. That's how Annenberg and Tunnel Tops were paid for.
   Philanthropy is the currency, and naming rights are what it buys. [judgment]

## Things that tie in

- **Park Presidio undergrounding (the Lake Street public comment, April 2026).** The same road one mile north, the
  same agencies, the same remedy. Contact its author and offer to file a joint comment asking SFCTA for **one
  Route 1 corridor study, Lincoln Way to the Presidio.** Two neighborhoods asking together is stronger than either
  alone. [judgment] Mountain Lake and its Pacific chorus frogs are a good second ecological story at that end.
- **The Geary/19th Avenue subway study.** SFCTA's strategic case (final report mid-2026) runs the line "south below
  Golden Gate Park" and on beneath 19th Avenue. It costs $20–30B and is 15–20 years out, with a $10–15M next phase
  and about $1.5M of Prop L already programmed. No alignment is fixed, and Crossover, SR-1 and Caltrans are not
  mentioned. [source] The tie-in is long-range. If the subway crosses under or near the Crossover corridor, its
  shafts, staging or a station near Fulton or Lincoln could share work with a lid. Ask SFCTA to note the Crossover
  corridor as a coordination point in the next phase. Don't oversell this.
- **Sunset Dunes and the Great Highway.** Prop K pushed traffic onto 19th Avenue and Chain of Lakes: about 2,000
  more cars a day on Chain of Lakes per SFMTA, and roughly 20% more northbound morning traffic on 19th per an SF
  Standard/Streetlight analysis. A recall followed. On November 3, 2026, **Prop G (weekday reopening) failed about
  66–34**, and even precincts along the Great Highway voted no. The fight is largely settled, but the traffic is
  real. **The single most important line in everything you send: this takes no lanes and diverts no traffic.**
  [source for traffic figures and Prop G; judgment on framing]
- **Chain of Lakes (Ben's idea, a possible concession to the west side).** Chain of Lakes Drive now carries the
  diverted traffic through the park. It meets JFK and MLK at crosswalks that are busy with people on foot, so cars
  queue and walkers wait. A grade-separated crossing at one or both intersections would help both. It's a bridge
  for people that also speeds cars, which is rare and politically useful. Two cautions:
  1. A faster Chain of Lakes invites more cut-through traffic. Rec & Park and the car-free advocates will notice,
     so present it as safety and a smoother flow at the crossings, not as capacity.
  2. It's a different road (city-owned, two lanes, no Caltrans), so it could move faster than anything on Crossover.
     It could be a first project that builds trust, or a distraction from the main one.

  This is a candidate for the same study, and a reason for Alan Wong to co-sponsor. Needs: Chain of Lakes
  volumes since the closure, and pedestrian counts at the JFK and MLK crossings. [judgment; open]
- **Golden Gate Park Access & Safety Program (SFMTA + Rec & Park, 2022).** The existing joint program for access
  inside the park. Its staff are the right people to brief at the working level. [source]
- **Caltrans District 4 Pedestrian Plan (2021) and Bay Area Bike Plan update (2025).** Check whether SR-1 in the
  park appears as a "location-based need." If it does, cite it: Caltrans has already said there's a gap. If it
  doesn't, ask to be added at the next update. [open]

   the sound meter.
6. **Hook to the Annenberg opening (December 2)** only after the per-square-foot comparison has some engineering
   behind it.

## What to avoid

- Leading with the tunnel or the full string of lids. Every planner will price the biggest thing you mention.
- Anything that reads as anti-car, or as Sunset Dunes, round two.
- Presenting [modeled] numbers as measured. Say "modeled" every time; it builds trust.
- Implying any agency endorsement. The footer already says citizen proposal; keep it that way.
- Fighting old fights in the pitch. Naming and history are for the essay, not the ask.
